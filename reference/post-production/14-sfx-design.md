# Sound Design (Phase 6 pass 3)

The delta over a generic SFX pass: this plugin already knows WHERE the video is set. Phase 1 wrote
`DOMAIN CONTEXT` from six location-qualified research queries, and Phase 3.5 wrote the cultural
research. A cue sheet can be derived from that instead of guessed.

Schemas in `10-post-production-pipeline.md`. Values in `global-promo-config.md` §29.3.
Tools: `tools/gen_sfx.py` (grow the library), `tools/mix_sfx.py` (print, mix, measure).

---

## 1. Deriving cues from the domain, not from the visuals alone

For each scene, three sources, in this order:

1. **`strategic-brief.md` > DOMAIN CONTEXT** — what machinery is actually in that room, what the
   workers actually do. A conveyor line in Cikarang sounds different from a port yard in Surabaya and
   different again from a control room. `amb-factory-floor` is not "factory sounds"; it is the bed
   this specific place has.
2. **Cultural research** — outdoor scenes carry the traffic, the birds, the call to prayer of that
   place, or they sound like stock footage of nowhere.
3. **The visual beat** — the cut, the reveal, the moment a number lands.

Cue types, function first: **motion** (whoosh) · **tension** (riser) · **emphasis** (impact, pop) ·
**snap** (click) · **ambience** (the bed under everything).

Ambience is the one this plugin can do better than a generic pass, and it is also the one that
carries most of the realism. Score it first, then the moments.

---

## 2. Library first, always

`media/sfx/library/` is the durable asset; each video is one draw from it.

- `palette.json` — recipes: generic id, prompt, duration, category, tags. **The source of truth for
  what the library SHOULD hold.**
- `catalog.json` — what EXISTS, with provenance per clip: source, model, licence, prompt.
- `clips/` — normalised audio. **Never committed.** Generated per install so nobody redistributes
  someone else's account's output.

Name recipes GENERICALLY. `whoosh-soft` gets reused for years; `whoosh-for-the-pelindo-intro` gets
used once and then re-generated by the next person who cannot tell what it contains.

Reuse before generating. `gen_sfx.py` only makes what the catalog lacks unless you pass `--force`.

---

## 3. Levels, and the numbers behind them

Every clip is normalised to **-20 LUFS with a -1.5 dBFS ceiling** so a plan's `gain_db` means the
same thing from one clip to the next.

| Situation | Gain |
|---|---|
| Long-form, transitions and whooshes | -12 to -18 dB |
| Short-form under near-continuous narration | transitions -3, story pops and impacts 0..+3, stamps and snaps 0..+7, layered risers 0..+2 |
| Percussive transients (knock, stamp, keys, snap) | **+3 to +5 dB above the table** |

That last row is not a preference. A short percussive clip hits the -1.5 dBFS peak ceiling **before**
it reaches -20 LUFS, so it catalogues quieter than a sustained clip at the same nominal loudness. Its
plan gain has to compensate or it disappears.

Short-form is its own calibration because the duck plus voice masking eat everything: under
continuous narration the long-form table is 4-8 dB too quiet across the board.

## 4. Verify audibility with numbers

After every mix, `mix_sfx.py` prints the dB each cue actually added over the voice-only reference.

| Cue kind | Must add |
|---|---|
| Story-critical | **≥ +4 dB** |
| Texture | +1 to +3 dB |

Four ways this measurement lies if you let it:

1. **A 0.6s window dilutes a 50ms click to nothing.** Transients are measured in ~0.3s. The tool
   picks the window from the cue id, so name a click like a click.
2. **An adjacent loud cue leaks into a wide window** and fakes a pass. In testing a click "measured"
   +9.8 dB because a chime 0.17s later shared its window.
3. **A riser is quiet at its onset.** Measure its final third, where its energy is.
4. **A cue sitting fully under continuous speech measures +0 dB at ANY gain.** Pushed to +9
   experimentally: identical. The duck suppresses it the whole time the voice is active. Accept it as
   felt-not-heard at a sane level and let it live in the word gaps, or delete it. **Never chase it
   with gain** — the moment a pause arrives it spikes.

---

## 5. Character beats level

A noisy TEXTURE cannot be fixed by lowering it. Static, glitchy or hummy clips read as NOISE over
speech even at -4 dB, and turning them down just produces quiet noise. This was rejected twice in
testing before the lesson stuck.

When the note is "that sounds noisy", swap the sound's CHARACTER — a clean mechanical snap instead of
a fizzy one — or use silence. Silence is part of the mix.

---

## 6. Density and the audit gate

**8 to 12 cues per minute.** Everything past that is `optional: true`, and `mix_sfx.py --no-optional`
drops exactly those. Mark liberally: the audit should be about the core set.

Score the signature moment of a beat, not every sub-frame. A three-shot click sequence is ONE
gesture, not three cues. Layer only the two or three biggest moments — riser into impact on a
scripted reveal, whoosh into pop so a cut lands.

**The user audits the cue sheet before anything is mixed.** `mix_sfx.py --print` produces it. This is
a hard gate, the same kind as the Phase 3.5 reference gate: not a formality, and not something to
skip because the sheet looks obviously fine.

---

## 7. Degradation

No ffmpeg: the cue sheet is still authored and printed, nothing is mixed, and the skill says so.
No API key: `gen_sfx.py` lists every recipe it could not make, with its prompt and duration, so the
sounds can be sourced elsewhere and added to the catalog by hand with their real licence.
